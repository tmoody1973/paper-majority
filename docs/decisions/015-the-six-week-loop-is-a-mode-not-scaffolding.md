# 015 — The six-week loop ships as a permanent mode, not as scaffolding

**Status:** Proposed by Claude on 2026-09-01. Decision pending Tarik.

- **Decision (proposed)** — The six-week playable core built for Checkpoint 2 is kept forever as a playable mode called **Session**, alongside the 24-week **Term**. Week 6 is designed as a real ending with its own record, not as a development checkpoint that later disappears.

- **Why this came up** — The gameplay review asked why anyone plays a second time. Runs that people replay hundreds of times are about forty minutes long and can end early: a bad run in Balatro or Slay the Spire dies and you start again. A Paper Majority term is 45 to 60 minutes, and because the design promises that failure continues the story (spec 3.5), a run that is clearly lost at week 10 must still be played out for half an hour. That is a tax on replay that the genre does not pay.

  The plan already builds a six-week loop as the Checkpoint 2 gate and then treats it as a stepping stone to 24 weeks. That throws away the one run length that fits a lunch break.

- **Options**
  1. **Six weeks as scaffolding only (current plan).** Nothing extra to maintain. The short, replayable shape of the game is discarded the moment the long one works.
  2. **Session and Term both ship (proposed).** Week 6 needs a genuine ending: pass or fail on the six-week objective, a Session Record, and outcomes that do not depend on an election that never happens. Two endings to maintain instead of one.
  3. **Session only; drop the 24-week term.** Cheapest to finish. Abandons the committee, chamber, Senate and reelection arc that is the game's civic reason to exist.

- **What we chose and why (recommendation)** — Option 2. The Checkpoint 2 build is being made anyway; the marginal cost is designing week 6 as an ending on purpose. It also gives the failure state the build brief now requires (Task 11: the six-week objective can visibly fail) a permanent home, and it gives first-time players an on-ramp that is not an hour long.

  Session outcomes should be a scaled version of the term's partial-outcome matrix: provisions drafted and docketed, coalition state reached, obligations kept or missed, and the explicit list of what was turned down. No election, no vote share; those belong to the Term.

- **What we gave up** — Two endings, two records, two balance targets. A risk that Session cannibalises Term, which is acceptable: Session is the tutorial that does not feel like one, and a player who only ever plays Session has still learned how a bill is built.

- **How we will know if this was right** — In Task 11's engagement gate, ask the five testers which they would rather play next: another six weeks, or a full term. If nobody wants the term, that is a problem with the term, not a reason to cut Session. If nobody wants another Session, this decision is wrong and the six-week loop is a tutorial after all.
