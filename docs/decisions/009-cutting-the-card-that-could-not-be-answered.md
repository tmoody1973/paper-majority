# 009 — The Urgent Renter Concern is cut. Supersedes 005.

- **Decision** — Remove `constituency-urgent-renter-concern` from the interaction
  spike. The desk now carries exactly **eight** card definitions across twelve
  instances, and every one of them takes part in a rule.

- **Why this came up** — Tarik was playing and asked why the card had gone grey with
  **Missed** stamped on it. Checking the data turned up three things at once:

  | | |
  |---|---|
  | It fits **no slot in any rule** | There was never a way to answer it |
  | Expiry applies **no penalty** | The only mention of District Trust in the engine is the 0–100 clamp |
  | It carries a **180-second countdown** | So it looks like both an action and a consequence |

  A card appeared with a ticking clock, could not be acted on, stamped itself Missed,
  and nothing happened. It was theatre.

  What is at stake: playtest measure 5 asks whether a player blames something they saw
  on the desk or just a number going down. A deadline the player cannot meet, for a
  penalty that does not exist, measures nothing — and reads as a bug.

- **Options**
  1. **Give it a job and a bite.** A fourth rule so a staffer can answer it, plus a
     District Trust drop on expiry. Real design — and it breaks "exactly three
     learnable patterns" and moves the 4-and-5 stack counts the checkpoint rests on.
  2. **Cut it (chosen).** Back to the eight definitions the plan's headline number
     already states. Gate unchanged at 4 base and 5 expanded.
  3. **Leave it.** Ship a countdown that punishes nobody and can be answered by nobody.

- **What we chose and why** — Option 2, by Tarik. It removes something fake instead of
  building more on top of it, and it makes the fixture match its own stated count.

  Measure 5 still has a real answer without it: taking the Ridgeline office's
  counteroffer costs **Policy Integrity**. That is a visible decision with a visible
  price, which is exactly the shape of answer the measure is looking for. The guide and
  worksheet now use that as the worked example.

- **What we gave up** — The spike no longer shows a deadline at all, so the "time
  pressure" half of the design's physical-pressure language goes untested until the
  weekly clock arrives in Task 7. Staff overload is the only remaining pressure cue.
  The engine's expiry mechanic still exists and is still covered by a domain test; it
  simply has no card using it in the spike.

- **What this corrects** — Decision 005 argued for keeping this card because it carried
  the only visible deadline. That reasoning never checked whether the deadline could be
  met. It could not. Recording the reversal rather than quietly editing 005.

- **How we will know if this was right** — In the playtest, does anyone reach measure 5
  and have nothing to point at? If several testers cannot name a cause because nothing
  bad ever happened to them, the spike needs option 1 after all — as Phase 2 work, not
  as a patch.
