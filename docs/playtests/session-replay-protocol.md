# Six-week Session — replay playtest protocol

**Status:** Prepared protocol. No participants have played under this protocol yet.

Use the implemented Session candidate only after its automated checks pass. Record the commit, snapshot ID/hash, seed, district, party, selected values and settings for every run. This is a small qualitative pilot, not a retention study representative of all players.

Before each participant begins, verify the page title is **Paper Majority**, record the exact URL, and export the frozen Session record after every completed run. Record keyboard-only or reduced-motion use as an observation; do not substitute an automated browser run for a participant.

Recruit eight adults who are interested in strategy games; include people unfamiliar with Congress. Explain what will be observed, obtain permission to keep anonymized notes, and let participants stop whenever they want. Do not record names, contact details or audio/video in the repository.

## First run

Say: “You run a congressional office. Build a housing proposal and get other offices behind it. Try the game, and tell me what you are thinking.” The game's own setup, guidance and controls must carry the rest. Do not teach a preferred strategy or reveal a recipe during the run. If you intervene because the interface is broken, record the intervention and exclude that run from independent-completion claims.

Observe the time to first accepted work, first bill provision, first explained sacrifice, first negotiation and ending. Record stretches of idle waiting, repeated explanations, unintelligible refusals, missed deadlines and physical input failures separately. Do not treat silence as satisfaction.

After Week 1, ask the participant to state the immediate and six-week objectives without looking at the objective panel. At the ending, ask:

1. What did you accomplish?
2. Which useful thing did you choose to give up, and why?
3. What happened later because of that choice?
4. Which action felt satisfying, and what on screen made it clear?
5. What would you try differently on another run?

## Voluntary second run

Offer: “You can try another run, or stop here.” Do not imply that replay is expected or reward it. Record actual starts and actual completions, not only stated willingness. Let the participant choose either the same challenge or a different seeded setup. Ask why they chose it.

Returning players should be able to reuse knowledge without replaying explanations. Observe whether they intentionally use a different evidence destination, staffing schedule, tactic, provision package or coalition path. A different random draw alone is not an intentional strategy change.

## Investigation thresholds

Proceed to broader testing when at least six of eight explain a sacrificed opportunity and its later consequence; at least five voluntarily start another run; at least four returning players complete it; and at least three returning players intentionally change approach. Report all numerators and denominators, including withdrawals and broken builds.

These are proposed product gates for finding problems. Failing one calls for a specific revision and a fresh observation; passing does not establish extensive replayability. Also retain original Checkpoint 2 requirements, including reviewed factual content, eight-card art pilot, keyboard completion and reduced motion.

## Recording a failure

Attach the setup and the smallest event/command trace that explains the issue. Classify it as input failure, unclear feedback, missing motivation, dominant strategy, unrecoverable supply, waiting/repetition, or unclear consequence. Name a proposed revision and the exact observation to repeat. Never fill the historical interaction-spike worksheet with these Session results.
